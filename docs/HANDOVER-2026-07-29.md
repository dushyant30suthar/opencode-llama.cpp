# Handover — 2026-07-29

Second day on the Qwen3.6-27B stack. Day one (see `HANDOVER-2026-07-28.md`)
moved from llama.cpp to a patched exllamav3 and claimed large speed wins.
**Most of those speed claims were a measurement artifact and are withdrawn
here.** What survives is a genuine caching advantage, a tuned config, two
upstream fixes, and a clear map of where this hardware's ceilings are.

Read this before touching anything: three separate measurement bugs produced
plausible-looking numbers in a single day. The rule that came out of it —
**trust the engine's own metrics, never a client-side stopwatch** — is now
enforced in the harnesses.

## 2. The measurement failures (read this first)

Three in one day, all producing believable numbers:

1. **Client-side token rate inflated ~1.7x.** `sweepx.py` divided all generated
   tokens by the window *after the first token arrived*, discarding
   time-to-first-token. On 91-token benchmark generations a real 74 t/s was
   reported as 125. llama.cpp's harness read llama-server's own timings and was
   correct, so **the cross-engine comparison was rigged by the measurement, not
   the engines**. Corrected tables are in `backends-qwen27b-benchmarks.md`
   behind the correction banner.
2. **A config key in the wrong YAML section is silently discarded.**
   `draft_num_tokens` under `model:` is dropped by pydantic (`extra='ignore'`);
   it belongs to `draft_model:`. A whole A/B sweep measured nothing and looked
   like a null result. `knob-ab.sh` now asserts the value is loaded before
   running.
3. **`/health` goes green against the previous server.** Killing the process
   isn't enough — TP workers linger and a half-dead server answers health while
   the next one is still loading. All harnesses now wait for **port 5000 to be
   free**, then require a real completion before measuring.

If a result looks surprising, suspect the harness before the engine.

## 3. Decode tuning — what moved and what didn't

All A/Bs used repeats with acceptance rates reported, because draft acceptance
independently swings throughput and will fake a result if left unmatched.

| knob | verdict | numbers |
|---|---|---|
| `cache_mode` Q8 → **6,4** | **adopted** | +7.7% @98k, +9.3% @131k, acceptance matched 77%/94-95%, spread at 131k [57.5-57.6] over 4 reps. Frees ~2.5 GB |
| `cache_mode` 4,4 | rejected | faster at 98k but loses at 131k and quantizes keys (the sensitive half) to 4 bits |
| `draft_num_tokens` | **keep 4** | 2 → 55.8 t/s @4k (-28%); 6 → 50.7 (-33%), acceptance collapses 72% → 59% |
| `tensor_parallel` | **keep true** | 74.5 vs 53.1 t/s @4k against layer split |
| `sysmem_recurrent_cache` 8192 → **4096** | **adopted** | swap 24 GB → 1 GB, RAM available 3 → 18 GB |
| `chunk_size` | **keep 2048** | 1024/2048/4096 = 531/548/553 t/s; 4096's +0.9% costs 320 MB of OOM margin |

### The counterintuitive one

**exllamav3 wants MORE KV quantization; llama.cpp wanted LESS.** On llama.cpp we
measured f16 keys halving the depth-degradation slope, because that engine
dequantizes as a separate step on every attention read. exllamav3 dequantizes
inside the attention kernel at no latency cost, so only bytes-read matters and
K6V4 > K8V8 > FP16 monotonically. **Do not carry cache settings between
engines.**

### Why the levers are what they are

Weights are 19.9 GB, 10 GB per GPU under TP, at 448 GB/s = 22 ms per decode
step = **45 t/s base ceiling**. Everything above that is MTP producing several
tokens per weight-read. So only two things raise the ceiling: fewer bytes per
read (quant / cache mode) or more tokens per read (draft window, already at its
optimum). A *larger* quant lowers it — 6 bpw would drop the ceiling to ~37 t/s.

## 4. Prefill — at the hardware limit, 26% of it unfixable

Cold 98k prefill is 543-553 t/s (~180 s), which is **parity with llama.cpp**.
The budget (PCIe probed on this box, rest derived):

- GPU0: CPU root port `00:01.0`, measured H2D 5.64 / D2H 4.32 GB/s (Gen3 x8;
  the 5060 Ti is a native x8 card, so this is not a misconfiguration).
- GPU1: `00:1c.0`, a **PCH port behind DMI 3.0**, measured H2D 2.85 / D2H
  2.76 GB/s. The ROG STRIX B360-F cannot bifurcate CPU lanes, so GPU1 **cannot
  be moved** to a CPU-attached slot on this board.
- TP fires `tp_reduce` once per attn/GDN module and once per MLP = **128
  all-reduces x 64 layers per forward**. Per rank per token: 1.31 MB out +
  1.31 MB in, **invariant to chunk_size** (only the call count changes).
- 98,304 tokens → 128.8 GB each direction per rank → **~47 s on GPU1's link =
  26% of wall time, a hard floor**.
- Residual ~134 s ≈ 25 TFLOP/s per GPU, about where EXL3 trellis-dequant GEMM
  should land on this card.

**≈74% compute-bound at hardware speed, ≈26% bound by a slot that can't be
changed. Realistic remaining headroom is 10-20%, not 2x.**

Other established facts:

- The MTP draft head **also prefills the whole prompt** (`job.py:1218`), on one
  GPU since `qwen3_5_mtp` declares `supports_tp: False` — costs ~5-8%. Keep it;
  MTP is worth 2-2.8x on decode.
- The 48 gated-deltanet layers are **cheap** in prefill (chunked scan, no
  quadratic term). They are why this model prefills as fast as it does; the
  quadratic cost is the 16 full-attention layers.
- **No prefill persistence exists** in either engine. A restart always costs a
  full reprocess. Harder to add here than llama.cpp's slot-save, because it
  would need both the paged K/V and the GDN recurrent checkpoints.

### Dead ends — verified, do not retry

`tensor_parallel_backend: nccl` (same PCIe bytes for 2 ranks; upstream #75
measured 438.2 vs 439.4 t/s) · `EXL3_BC_ATTN` (decode-only, declines under TP) ·
`EXL3_PREFER_FA2` (flash-attn not installed) · `EXL3_TP_REDUCE_THREADS`
(AVX-512 only; i5-9400F is AVX2) · moving GPU1 to another slot (B360 limitation).

## 5. Context capacity — the number that matters most

The cache is a **paged, content-hashed pool**: each turn appends pages and
reuses everything already resident. Today's log shows **97.6% prefix cache hit
rate over 787 requests**. `cache_size` is not a fill target — it is the ceiling
on everything resident *simultaneously*, and when it fills, LRU pages are
evicted, which is what forces a rebuild.

`max_seq_len` (196608) caps a single conversation; `cache_size` caps the sum.

| cache_size | holds | practical |
|---|---|---|
| 196608 | one max conversation | always evicting a second |
| **262144** (current) | one 196k + 65k | 2 sessions at ~130k |
| 294912 | one 196k + 98k | 2 at ~147k |
| 327680 | one 196k + 131k | 2 at ~163k, or 3 at ~109k |

**Sizing constraint:** prefill needs **1.2-2.2 GB transient VRAM on top of the
cache**. `cache_size 360448` loaded fine, reported healthy, and then OOM'd on
every prefill with 53 MiB free. Always accept a new size with a **cold 98k
prefill**, never with `/health` — `scripts/cache-max.sh` does exactly that.

## 6. Upstream contributions

Both fixes from `docs/exllamav3-upstream-report.md` are merged into
exllamav3 `dev`:

- `ce77f0a` — GDN batched rewind launched all layers' jobs on one device;
  illegal memory access on any multi-GPU split with MTP. (Our patch.)
- `1048003` — requeue check preceded the EOS check, so a job finishing at
  `max_new_tokens` was requeued into a zombie with negative budget that held a
  recurrent state slot forever. Upstream placed the fix in
  `Job.receive_sample()`, which also covers the non-draft path — better than
  our version.

Issue #260 carries the analysis; PR #262 was closed as superseded.

## 7. Standing operating notes

- **One engine at a time.** llama.cpp and exllamav3 both want the two GPUs.
- **Client env for agentic use:** `API_TIMEOUT_MS=600000` prevents a client
  abandoning a long rebuild (an abandoned rebuild is wasted entirely — the
  retry starts over). `CLAUDE_CODE_ATTRIBUTION_HEADER=0`,
  `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_AUTO_MEMORY=1`,
  `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_BACKGROUND_TASKS=1` reduce early-context churn.
- **`tool_format: qwen3_coder` is required.** Without it the model's XML tool
  calls stream through as plain text and agentic clients show raw blocks
  instead of executing them.
- **Compact by ~90-120k.** Every rebuild costs depth ÷ ~550 t/s seconds, and
  the top of the window is where both stalls and the decode tax live.
- The one remaining speed/quality trade is **4.00 bpw**: ~+10-13% decode for a
  measured 2.3x mean KL increase (0.035 → 0.080; noise floor 0.0102). EXL3
  4.0 bpw still beats GGUF Q4_K_M on KL at 3 GiB smaller. Not taken — it is a
  quality decision, not a tuning one.
